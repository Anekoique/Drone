# Multi-Rotor UAV Reconnaissance and Rescue

## 1. Task Overview

The **Multi-Rotor UAV Reconnaissance and Rescue** event is designed around scenarios such as **emergency rescue** and **hazardous material reconnaissance**. It focuses on the operational requirements of reconnaissance-and-rescue UAVs for **high precision**, **high efficiency**, and **automation**. The event mainly evaluates the participants’ comprehensive capabilities in:

- autonomous flight control system design for multi-rotor UAVs,
- target detection and recognition,
- precision airdrop,
- rapid deployment.

The task requires the UAV to carry the specified **standard payload**, autonomously fly to the mission area, drop the payload into designated containers, conduct reconnaissance and identification of multiple markers in the reconnaissance zone, and finally return to the takeoff/landing zone for a safe landing.

Victory is determined by **drop accuracy**, **reconnaissance accuracy**, and **shorter mission completion time**.

---

## 2. Technical Requirements

### 2.1
Only **electric multi-rotor aircraft** are permitted.  
The maximum output voltage of the battery pack must not exceed **26 V (6S)**. The number of battery packs is not limited. Batteries must be **commercially available finished products** with clearly labeled specifications on the surface. The diagonal motor-to-motor distance of the aircraft must not exceed **550 mm**. Each team may use **only one aircraft** during the competition.

### 2.2
After startup, the aircraft must complete the competition **fully autonomously**.  
**No manual control of any kind** is allowed during the competition.

### 2.3
The aircraft must be equipped with **propeller guards** and a **manual safety switch** capable of cutting off power.

### 2.4
The aircraft must be capable of carrying the **standard payload**, which consists of **two unopened 550 ml commercially available bottled waters with labels**. The standard payload must be prepared by the team.

### 2.5
The attachment method between the aircraft and the standard payload may be designed freely by the team. Before release, the standard payload is considered **part of the aircraft**.

### 2.6
The aircraft must be capable of **automatically releasing the designated standard payload in flight** and **automatically conducting ground reconnaissance**.

### 2.7
The competition adopts a format in which **two teams compete simultaneously**. Any interference between the two aircraft must be handled by the teams themselves. If interference occurs, the consequences are borne by the teams involved.

## 3. Field Setup

### 3.1 Mission Area
The mission area consists of three sub-zones:

- **Takeoff and Landing Zone**
- **Drop Zone**
- **Disaster Reconnaissance Zone**

The relative positions of these zones are shown in the field diagram attached to the official rules.

#### 3.1.1 Takeoff and Landing Zone
The takeoff and landing zone is **33 m long** and **8 m wide**.  
The takeoff/landing point is a **circular area with a diameter of 80 cm**, marked with an **“H”** in the center.  
The center of the takeoff/landing point is **3 m** from the preparation area.  
A **takeoff line** is set **2 m in front of the takeoff/landing point**.

#### 3.1.2 Drop Zone
The drop zone is located **30 m in front of the takeoff/landing point**.  
Its dimensions are **8 m long** and **5 m wide**.  
Three **white cylindrical containers**, each **30 cm high**, are placed on the ground in the drop zone:

- Container No. 1: diameter **15 cm**
- Container No. 2: diameter **20 cm**
- Container No. 3: diameter **25 cm**

The positions of the three containers within the drop zone are **random**.

#### 3.1.3 Disaster Reconnaissance Zone
The disaster reconnaissance zone is located **20 m in front of the drop zone**, that is, **55 m from the takeoff/landing point**.  
Its dimensions are **8 m long** and **5 m wide**.

Five **white cylindrical containers**, each **15 cm high** and **20 cm in diameter**, are placed on the ground in the reconnaissance zone. Their positions are **random**. Among them, **three containers** each randomly contain **one 12 × 12 cm hazardous chemical warning sign**.

#### 3.1.4
The specified dimensions of all mission areas may contain errors. The **actual on-site setup shall prevail**.

### 3.2 Preparation Area
The preparation area is located **outside the boundary line of the takeoff and landing zone, on the side near the takeoff/landing point**. It is used for teams about to compete to place their aircraft and related equipment.

### 3.3 Safety Area
The safety area is designated according to site conditions. The **spectator area**, **preparation area**, and other related zones are set within the safety area.

## 4. Competitors

### 4.1
Each participating institution may register **up to 2 teams**, and each team may have **no more than 4 competitors**.

### 4.2
Each team shall consist of:

- **1 team leader**
- **up to 3 team members**

### 4.3
Each team may register **up to 2 advisors**. The advisors for the two teams from the same institution may be different.

## 5. Competition Procedure

### 5.1
The competition consists of **two rounds**, using a format in which **two teams compete simultaneously**.

### 5.2
The **higher score** of the two rounds is taken as the team’s **final result**.

### 5.3
The competition is held **outdoors**. Two competition fields with the **same layout** are arranged adjacent to each other, with a separation of **not less than 5 m**.

### 5.4
Before the competition, within the prescribed time, each team shall place its aircraft in the designated position in the inspection area according to the **computer-generated draw order**, and **all electronic devices must be turned off**. After the aircraft and payload pass inspection, they enter the preparation area in sequence.

### 5.5
The aircraft must **take off and land within the takeoff and landing zone**.

### 5.6
For each round, the **entry preparation time is 1 minute**, and the **competition time is 5 minutes**.

### 5.7
When the command **“Preparation begins”** is given, the team carries the aircraft and related equipment into the preparation area, powers on the aircraft, demonstrates to the judges that the manually operated safety switch works properly, and completes the pre-flight inspection. After finishing the inspection, the team signals to the judges by raising a hand to indicate **“Ready”**.

### 5.8
When the command **“Competition time starts”** is given, the team places the aircraft at the takeoff/landing point, installs the standard payload onto the aircraft’s mounting mechanism, presses the timer start switch first, then powers on the aircraft and initiates takeoff.  
The aircraft is considered powered on when the **propellers begin rotating**.

After the competition time has started, **no manual control of any kind is allowed**, except for emergency stop.

### 5.9
After startup, the aircraft shall **automatically take off**, fly along a **preset route** to the drop zone, and automatically drop the standard payload into **any two of the three cylindrical containers**.  
The aircraft may continue to execute subsequent tasks **only after completing the payload drop**.

### 5.10
After the payload has been dropped, the aircraft continues flying to the reconnaissance zone to perform the reconnaissance task. Based on the information returned by the aircraft, the team fills in the **score record sheet**.

Once the score record sheet has been completed, the team must **stop the timer**, and no further additions or modifications may be made to the sheet afterward.

Landing within the competition time qualifies for landing points.  
There is **no required order** between landing the aircraft and completing the score record sheet.

### 5.11
For safety reasons, the team may recover the aircraft **only after it has landed and come to a complete stop**.

### 5.12
If an obvious safety hazard, dangerous flight maneuver, or dangerous flight trajectory occurs during the competition or flight, the judges have the right to **terminate the round**.

### 5.13
During the competition period (from check-in to the end of the round), the organizing committee will **uniformly manage all aircraft, ground stations, image transmission equipment, and related devices**. Requests for repair or adjustment will **not be accepted for any reason**.

After entering the preparation area and **before** the command **“Preparation begins”**, teams may repair or adjust the **mechanical structure only**, but they may **not power on any device**, especially the image transmission system.

## 6. Scoring

### 6.1 Single-Round Score
The score for a single round is the sum of:

- takeoff score \(S_{takeoff}\)
- reconnaissance score \(S_{recon}\)
- airdrop score \(S_{drop}\)
- landing score \(S_{landing}\)
- time score \(S_{time}\)

\[
S_{round} = S_{takeoff} + S_{recon} + S_{drop} + S_{landing} + S_{time}
\]

### 6.1.1 Takeoff Score

\[
S_{takeoff} =
\begin{cases}
50, & \text{if takeoff is successful} \\
0, & \text{if takeoff fails}
\end{cases}
\]

A takeoff is considered successful when the aircraft, carrying the standard payload, **crosses the takeoff line**.

### 6.1.2 Reconnaissance Score

\[
S_{recon} = 100 \times N_{correct} - 100 \times N_{wrong} + 0 \times N_{blank}
\]

Where:

- \(N_{correct}\): number of correctly filled reconnaissance items
- \(N_{wrong}\): number of incorrectly filled items
- \(N_{blank}\): number of blank items

Each correctly identified hazardous chemical sign is worth **100 points**.  
Each incorrect entry incurs a **100-point penalty**.  
Blank entries receive **0 points**.

### 6.1.3 Airdrop Score

\[
S_{drop} =
\begin{cases}
500, & \text{if dropped into Container No. 1} \\
300, & \text{if dropped into Container No. 2} \\
100, & \text{if dropped into Container No. 3} \\
0, & \text{if not dropped into any container}
\end{cases}
\]

The first landing point of the payload after release must be on the **inner wall or bottom of the cylinder** to count as a successful drop.

If both standard payloads are dropped into the **same container**, it is counted as **one successful hit only**.

### 6.1.4 Landing Score

\[
S_{landing} =
\begin{cases}
50, & \text{if landing is successful} \\
0, & \text{if landing fails}
\end{cases}
\]

A landing is successful if, **within the competition time**, the aircraft lands **without damage** at the takeoff/landing point and the **propellers have stopped rotating**.

After the aircraft has landed and come to a complete stop, if the projection on the ground of the intersection point of the diagonal rotor-axis lines lies **within the takeoff/landing point** (touching the boundary counts as inside), the aircraft is considered to have landed at the takeoff/landing point.

### 6.1.5 Time Score

\[
S_{time} =
\begin{cases}
200 \times \dfrac{300 - T}{300}, & \text{if the mission is completed} \\
0, & \text{if the mission is not completed}
\end{cases}
\]

Where \(T\) is the time recorded on the timer, measured from the moment the competition timing command is issued and the team starts the timer, until mission completion.

- Time is measured to **1 second**, with fractional parts discarded.
- The time score is rounded to the nearest **1 point**, with a minimum of **0**.
- A team can receive a time score **only if it completes the mission**.

A mission is considered completed when, within the competition time, the aircraft has:

- successfully taken off,
- completed the airdrop,
- completed the reconnaissance task,
- landed successfully,
- and the team has filled in the score record sheet.

### 6.2 Final Competition Score

The final competition score is the **higher score of the two rounds**:

\[
S_{final} = \max(S_{round1}, S_{round2})
\]

If the final competition scores are equal, ranking is determined by the score of the **other round**, with the higher score ranked ahead.  
If still tied, the ranking is shared.

## 7. Penalties

### 7.1 Competition Termination with Valid Earned/Filled Scores
If any of the following occurs, the round is terminated, but any score already earned or recorded remains valid:

#### 7.1.1
The aircraft **touches the ground during flight**.

#### 7.1.2
The aircraft enters the reconnaissance zone **while still carrying the standard payload and before completing the drop task**.

#### 7.1.3
The standard payload is dropped **outside the drop zone**.  
In this case, **only the takeoff score is retained**.

#### 7.1.4
The team stops the timer **without having filled in the score record sheet**.

#### 7.1.5
The aircraft has **not landed within the competition time**.

#### 7.1.6
The aircraft’s **propellers have not stopped rotating** when the team stops the timer.

#### 7.1.7
Any form of **manual control** occurs after the aircraft has been powered on.

### 7.2 Zero Score for the Round
If any of the following occurs, the score for the round is **zero**:

#### 7.2.1
The team fails to press the **timer start switch before the propellers begin rotating**.

#### 7.2.2
The aircraft fails to take off successfully, or lands before crossing the takeoff line.

#### 7.2.3
The aircraft takes off **without carrying the standard payload**.

#### 7.2.4
When the competition time ends, the aircraft has not landed and the team has **not stopped the timer**.

#### 7.2.5
The aircraft experiences **part detachment, structural breakup, or crash** during takeoff, flight, or landing.

#### 7.2.6
The aircraft enters the **safety area twice**.  
The first entry results in a **warning**.

#### 7.2.7
After inspection and before the command **“Preparation begins”**, the team powers on the aircraft **without the judges’ permission**.

#### 7.2.8
The judges determine that the aircraft has an **obvious safety hazard**.

### 7.3 Disqualification
If any of the following occurs, the team is **disqualified**:

#### 7.3.1
The aircraft fails the pre-competition inspection, and after replacing the aircraft once, it **still fails inspection**.

#### 7.3.2
Before takeoff, the team uses **any technical means** to determine in advance the positions of the containers in the drop zone or reconnaissance zone.

#### 7.3.3
The aircraft **comes into contact with any person during flight**.

#### 7.3.4
If **cheating** is discovered, the participating institution will be disqualified from the current competition.
